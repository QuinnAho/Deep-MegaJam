# Devlog

Raw material for the Glass Box prize, which goes to the best dev log showing how the game was built. This note is the honest record: what we did, what went wrong and what we cut. The devlog-scribe agent turns it into posts. Nothing here is polished and nothing is left out because it is embarrassing.

## Entry format

Add entries as things happen, newest at the bottom of the day.

```
### 9:40 PM  Short title
- What happened: one or two sentences.
- Why it matters: what it changed, or what we learned.
- Evidence: a link, a screenshot path, a commit hash or a test result.
```

Times are EST. Mark a time "about" when it comes from memory and not from a timestamp.

### 9:27 PM  The Director gets a voice, as data
- What happened: The director-writer agent writes all of the Director's lines as two CSV files.   - "Please hold your breath. Your air is important to us." (also the cut)
  - "Of all my divers, you were easily the most recent." (the win)

### 10:40 PM  The sea serpent exists, and hears you through Unreal's own AI
- What happened: The monster is a sea serpent built from about 20 instanced engine spheres that follow the path its head actually swam, tapering to the tail with a travelling side-to-side weave. It has two unlit glowing eyes, so you see a pair of points in the dark before you see the body. No animation assets. Halfway through, Quinn asked whether it could use Unreal's game AI. Now the serpent is a Pawn possessed by an `AAIController`, and it hears through the engine's perception system with the hearing sense: winching, sprinting and grabbing turn into engine noise events. The decision is still one pure, tested function with five states. Lurk circles beyond lamp range. Stalk spirals in on the tether when noise passes 60. Bite holds the line for a 5 s alarm. Recoil happens when the lamp is held on its head for 0.75 s. Hunt happens only if you have no tether. We kept it off Behavior Trees, StateTree assets and the navmesh: those are binary assets we cannot test headless or merge, and this is open 3D water. The work is purely additive under `Monster\` so it merges with the branch that adds the cut and death, which will bind to its events.
- Why it matters: The monster is fair by construction, and the tests prove it: it never severs a tether without the full 5 s alarm, the beam always breaks a bite at once, and silence always calms it. Gotchas found: a bare test world has no AI system (`World->CreateAISystem()`), and perception delivers a noise only once world time has moved past it, so the test advances `World->TimeSeconds` itself. The harness on `main` still refuses to build while Quinn's editor is open on another checkout. The fix is on `dev` (e374c6b), so this run used a temporary copy of that script.
- Evidence: `verify` gives 20 of 20 tests passed (5 new: `Deep.Sim.Monster.Noise/Beam/States/Steering`, `Deep.World.Monster`). Report in `deep\Saved\Harness\test-20261114-223925-report\index.json` on branch `dev/monster`. Nobody has seen it move yet: the weave, the eyes and the approach are unverified by eye.

(5) Restraint: Dead Space's lesson as the articles summarise Ignacio's GDC talk and the Giant Bomb "Markers" essays, that the screen stays clear and information appears where and when it is needed, with the caveat that a fully diegetic HUD is often less readable; so aboard is diegetic-style tags, and the water keeps four small non-diegetic panels.

### 9:40 AM  The silt's blocks and streaks: what they were and the recipe (polish/ascent)
- What happened: the block pattern on the pit floor at 1 m is in `M_Silt`'s own code, not a texture: its grain was a hash of `floor(position x 40)` (colour) and `floor(position x 60)` (normal), square cells of 2.5 and 1.7 cm with hard edges, which read as blocks once the lamp is near. And every term used world X and Y only, a projection from straight above, so on the sinkhole's steep walls the pattern stretched into vertical streaks.
- Looked up: Epic's `material_basics` skill (EditorToolset, read from the engine's plugin folder): reuse an existing material or function before writing graph logic. The engine's triplanar functions (`WorldAlignedTexture`, `WorldAlignedNormal`) need a texture and we have none for silt; their method is what we copy: project on the three world planes and blend by the surface normal's components raised to a power (80.lv, "UE5 triplanar deep dive, from WorldAlignedTexture to high-quality normals", part 1). The same article warns that normals sampled per plane and blended without reorienting light wrong on curved or steep shapes; the forum threads on stretched slopes (forums.unrealengine.com, "How to avoid stretching of UVs on steep angles", "Stretching on some parts of world aligned texture") say a single projection will always stretch somewhere.
- The recipe: grain is smooth value noise in 3D (trilinear with a smoothstep, a sine-free hash so large world coordinates do not band), so there are no cells and no projection to stretch; octaves at 25 cm, 8 cm, 3.3 cm, 1.2 cm for colour and 4.5 cm, 1.6 cm, 7 mm for the bump, so there is detail at 0.5 m, fading with distance so it does not shimmer far off. The normal is built in world space (the material's tangent-space normal is off): the gradient of a height of a few millimetres by central differences, plus the sand ripples projected on three planes and blended by the vertex normal (a third of the ripple on walls), with the gradient taken along the surface only. ### 3:10 PM  The serpent's body is a rope the head drags (polish/serpent-look)
- What happened: Quinn, 2:40 PM: "instead of a slither mechanic we need each spline to have physics so that it moves like an actual snake in 3d space ... the controller is the head". The sideways sine weave is gone. The body is now a chain of particles: particle 0 is pinned to the head, and the rest are pure physics in `UDeepMonsterRules::StepChain`. Mid-task Quinn added: "thers a lot of mmovment it should be zero gravity rope physics im owonering if the animation is still playing". It was: the head still added a small swimming stroke. The stroke is now 0, sideways drag went up, and the pull toward the trail changed from a spring to a drift.
- Recipe:
  1. Verlet: `Vel = Pos - Old`, damped by `exp(-drag*h)`. No gravity. Water drag is 1.2/s along the body and 10/s more across it, so the body slides along its length but does not swing sideways.
  2. Fixed substeps of 1/120 s with a carried remainder. Frames are clamped at 0.1 s.
  3. Jakobsen relaxation, 4 passes. Each pass moves both ends of a link half way to its rest length. The pinned neck takes no correction.
  4. Then one head-to-tail follow-the-leader pass. It clamps each joint to the bend limit (28 degrees) and sets the exact link length.
  5. A weak drift onto the head's trail (0.5/s). Old moves with Pos, so the drift adds no speed and never overshoots. A cave keep holds every particle `CaveWallClearM` off the rock.
  6. The flinch is a cosine impulse along the body, written into Old. The chain resets on spawn, teleport and whole-trail snap.
  7. The tube's centreline is a uniform Catmull-Rom curve through the particles (`ChainPoint`), so the joints show no creases.
- Sources: Epic's `CableComponent.cpp`, which I read in the engine source: `VerletIntegrate` near line 791 and the substep loop near line 1216, where I took the fixed substep and carry. Thomas Jakobsen, "Advanced Character Physics", GDC 2001, used from memory and not fetched: Verlet with distance relaxation and pinned particles.
- Failures on the way:

  - My first try at a drift pull let the body fold back on itself after a 90 degree turn. A spring at 1.5/s fixed that, but it swung, and Quinn saw the swing. The drift at 0.5/s, with the heavy sideways drag, passes the same turn test.
